Where the ring hunters go wrong

Each neighborhood is 60 transfers at most. 20 of 60 hold an IBM-labeled laundering ring; 40 are ordinary traffic. A model is right when it calls the ring and flags at least half its transfers, and wrong twice over when it calls an ordinary neighborhood a ring. The charts read from the bench's own scoring files at 12:57.

Every model on the main slice: rings found against false alarms

The top left corner is the goal. Most families sit to the right: they find most rings by calling nearly everything a ring. Only the versions with code facts and a confidence rule move left, and they give up one to three rings to do it.

Jev as a second check on Sonnet's ring calls

Jev scores each neighborhood's structure in a tenth of a second. Keeping a Sonnet ring call only when Jev's score clears a bar set on the main slice removes half or more of the false alarms on every slice. Rings found after the gate: 13 of 13 on main, 8 of 10 on heldout A, 12 of 13 on heldout B.
